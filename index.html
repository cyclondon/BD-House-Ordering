    Your first quote

    1. Ask the customer what the opening is for, roughly how big it is, and the finish they like. Most trade customers know what they want; homeowners need the product guide below.
    2. In Quote builder, fill in the customer on the right first. Returning customers fill in automatically when you pick their name.
    3. For each opening: pick the product, set the location (floor, side of building, room, e.g. "GF REAR – Kitchen"), type the measured size in mm, choose supply only or supply & fit, then colour, handing and glass, and press Add to quote. Floor, side and supply carry over to the next product, so a whole house goes quickly.
    4. Set Customer type: trade customers see prices ex VAT; homeowners see prices inc VAT, like a retail quote.
    5. Press Save quote. It now appears in Quotes & orders on this device. Export to Excel regularly to back up and share.
    6. Press Customer quote, then Download PDF, and email the PDF. Set the status to Sent. The factory order sheet has its own Download PDF too.
    7. When the customer accepts and signs the drawings, press Convert to order, take the deposit, then send the Factory order sheet.

    To practise without touching real data, go back to Quote builder and press Load a practice quote.

    Measuring

    • Measure in millimetres. Measure the width at the top, middle and bottom, and the height at the left, middle and right. Use the smallest of each.
    • Choose "The opening I measured" and the page takes off a fitting gap (10mm wide, 5mm high by default) so the frame slides in. The factory sheet shows both sizes.
    • Choose "The exact frame size" only when the customer or fitter gives you a finished frame size.
    • For glass, enter the exact finished glass size. No gap is taken off.
    • Check the opening is square: if the two diagonals differ by more than 5mm, note it in "Notes for factory" and tell the fitter.

    Handing (which way it opens)

    Always stand on the room side, inside, facing the door. The factory reads every drawing from the inside (內看).

    • Hinged left / right: which side the hinges are on.
    • Opens in: the door swings towards you. Opens out: away from you.
    • On the drawing the triangle points at the hinges. A solid triangle opens in; a dashed one opens out.
    • For sliders, say which panel is on the outer track. For bifolds, say which side the leaves stack to.

    Choosing glass

    • Doors and low glazing must be safety glass (toughened or laminated). Never quote plain float glass in a door.
    • 6mm clear toughened is the safe default. Frosted (acid-etched) for bathrooms and privacy. Low-iron for a clearer, less green look.
    • IGU (double glazing) for warmth and sound between heated and unheated rooms.
    • Products marked "glass included" already have it in the price.
    • Check the frame takes the glass. Each frame has a maximum glass thickness. Standard double glazing (5+12A+5) is 22mm, which is too thick for the 20mm frames, so the page offers 19mm and 16mm units for them. Options that don't fit are greyed out.

    How prices work

    Our selling prices come from the UK price book, set by checking what UK suppliers charge and pricing below them to win trade business. You never need to work out a price yourself.

    • Doors, sliders and windows include 6mm clear toughened glass. Choosing a different glass, or extras such as locks and closers, adds to the price automatically.
    • Many products are priced per square metre with a minimum: small items cost the minimum.
    • Hinged doors include a set size; bigger doors add a charge per extra m².
    • A yellow warning means the size is outside the factory's limits. Don't send it; ask Calvin for a factory price.
    • "Factory cost & margin" under the price is for managers. It never appears on the customer quote.

    Supply & fit and payment

    • Supply only is our normal trade sale. Supply & fit means our installer partner fits it: choose remove & replace when an old door or window comes out, or into prepared opening for new builds and extensions.
    • The page suggests a fitting price from the guide rates in Pricing settings. Type the fitter's real figure once they have surveyed.
    • The customer quote prints a payment schedule (50% deposit, 40% before dispatch, 10% on delivery or completion by default), what happens next, delivery and site requirements, a page per item with views from inside and outside, and a confirmation for the customer to sign.
    • Tick which pages to attach under Pages to attach in the quote: the customer journey (what happens from deposit to aftercare), the site and delivery guide (access, unloading, storage, failed visits, with a signature box) and the payment terms and conditions. They adapt to supply only or supply & fit, and to trade or homeowner. The fees in them come from Pricing settings.
    • Don't order from the factory until the signed confirmation and deposit are in.

    Showing colours and finishes

    • The drawing has two views. Finish view shows the frame in the chosen colour and finish (anodised sheen, matt powder coat, sand texture, woodgrain) with the glass as it looks (clear, frosted, grey tint, double glazing). Line drawing is the technical view the factory works from.
    • Where several colours share a price, pick the exact colour so the sample, quote and factory sheet all match.
    • The customer quote shows the finish view from inside and outside with colour and glass samples. The factory sheet keeps the line drawing and adds the samples.
    • Screen and print colours are only a guide. For any order that depends on colour, send a physical sample and get it approved before the customer signs.

    Common mistakes

    • Handing read from the wrong side. Always inside, facing the door.
    • Entering inches or centimetres. Sizes are mm: a standard door is about 2100 high.
    • Forgetting the glass on "unglazed" products, or forgetting locks and closers in Extras.
    • Sending to the factory before the customer has signed. Once cutting starts, changes are charged in full.
    • Promising stock dates. Custom items go in the next container: quote the lead time on the customer quote.

    Glossary

    Leaf (門玉 / 窗玉)
    The opening part of a door or window. A double door has two leaves.
    Frame (框)
    The fixed outer part that is fixed to the wall.
    Transom (門頭)
    A panel above the door, either fixed glass or an opening window.
    Fixed light (梗位)
    A glass panel that doesn't open.
    Sash
    The opening part of a window.
    Series (e.g. 50 series)
    The aluminium profile size in mm. Bigger number = chunkier, stronger frame.
    Toughened glass (鋼化)
    Heat-treated safety glass that breaks into small pieces. Can't be cut after toughening.
    Laminated glass (夾膠)
    Two sheets bonded with a plastic layer that holds the glass together if it breaks.
    IGU / double glazing (中空)
    Two panes with a sealed gap (e.g. 12A = 12mm air gap).
    Low-iron / ultra-clear (超白)
    Glass without the green tint.
    Floor spring (地較)
    A closer set into the floor so a door swings both ways and closes itself.
    Bifold (摺疊門)
    Doors that fold and stack to one side to open a whole wall.
    Lift-slide (提昇)
    Hardware that lifts a heavy sliding panel so it glides, then seals down when closed.
    Multipoint lock (多點鎖)
    A lock that bolts at several points for security and seal.
    Powder coated (噴粉 / 噴白)
    A painted finish. Anodised (銀白, 電黑) is a metallic finish.
    Sq ft (呎²)
    Square feet: the factory prices by area. 1 m² ≈ 10.76 sq ft.
    Landed cost
    What the item costs us delivered to the UK, after freight and duty.
